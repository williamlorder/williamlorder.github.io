Covariates and three-step methods

Auxiliary variables can enter a mixture model in two ways:

  • one-step: the covariates are part of the model (lca(...; covariates = X), Mplus c ON x), estimated jointly with the measurement model;
  • three-step (Vermunt 2010; Asparouhov & Muthén 2014): the measurement model is fitted first, cases are assigned to their most likely class, and the auxiliary model is estimated with the classification error held fixed — r3step for covariates (Mplus R3STEP), bch for continuous distal outcomes (Mplus BCH) and dcat for categorical distal outcomes (Mplus DCAT, Lanza, Tan & Bray 2013).

Three-step methods keep the class definitions from changing when auxiliary variables are added, which is usually what one wants when the classes are meant to be measured by the indicators alone.

The examples use the aces data (see Example datasets): eight adverse childhood experiences of 1,500 adults generated from four classes whose membership depends on sex, age and parental education, plus three outcomes that depend on the class only — depressive symptoms (depress, 0–24), self-rated health (srh, 1 excellent … 5 poor) and smoking.

using LatentMixtures

Y = d[:, r"^ace_"]
m = lca(Y, 4)

One-step latent class regression

The class shares are generated by a multinomial logit with Low adversity as reference, in which low_parent_edu has coefficients 0.6, 0.5 and 1.0 (Household, Maltreatment, High), female 0, 0.3 and 0.2, and age (per 10 years) −0.05, −0.10, −0.10.

X = d[:, [:female, :age, :low_parent_edu]]
m1 = lca(Y, 4; covariates = X, ref = 1)

Sampling weights

The sample over-represents adults whose parents had no post-secondary education (45% against 30% in the target population), who are more often in the adversity classes; weight undoes the oversampling. Weights are rescaled to sum to the number of analysed rows, as in Mplus; the log-likelihood, class proportions, classification tables, standard errors, R3STEP and BCH are all weighted.

mw = lca(Y, 4; weights = d.weight)
(unweighted = round.(classprobs(m); digits = 3), weighted = round.(classprobs(mw); digits = 3))
(unweighted = [0.56, 0.186, 0.156, 0.099], weighted = [0.589, 0.178, 0.146, 0.088])

The weighted shares are closer to the population shares (0.55, 0.19, 0.16, 0.10 in generating order Low, Household, Maltreatment, High).

Which method?

goalfunctionMplus
covariates as part of the modellca/lpa/growth(...; covariates)c ON x
covariates, measurement fixedr3stepAUXILIARY = x (R3STEP)
continuous distal outcomebchAUXILIARY = y (BCH)
categorical distal outcomedcatAUXILIARY = y (DCAT)
propagate measurement uncertainty into your own estimatormeasurement_estimates, posterior_at–

Modal assignment followed by an ordinary regression on the assigned classes (“classify-analyse”) attenuates the associations; the three-step corrections above remove that bias (Bolck, Croon & Hagenaars 2004; Vermunt 2010).
