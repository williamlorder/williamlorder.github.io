Example: ACE classes in the BRFSS

Latent class analysis of the eight adverse childhood experience (ACE) categories of the Behavioral Risk Factor Surveillance System 2022–2024 (CDC; public domain): 136,947 adults in the 24 state-years (15 states) with ACE module answers, analysed with the BRFSS final weights. The scripts, the shipped extract (examples/brfss_ace/data/, 1.65 MB) and exact reproduction steps are in examples/brfss_ace/README.md; the causal analysis of the same data (interventions on abuse, class-specific effects, sensitivity to a direct class effect) is the BRFSS example of LatentClassCausal.jl.

What the example covers:

  • class enumeration as a Slurm array (one task per K = 1..6, 2000/400 random starts): fitstats, convergence, lmr on the weighted fits, blrt (96 replications per K) on unweighted fits, because blrt does not take sampling weights;
  • the four-class solution: itemprobs, classprobs, classification_table;
  • covariates of class membership with r3step and adult outcomes by class with bch, both weighted.

Survey design. lca(...; weights) gives pseudo-maximum-likelihood estimates and robust standard errors that ignore BRFSS strata and PSUs; the scripts contain a marked design = (;) block that passes cluster/strata once lca accepts them. Until then the standard errors below are too small for design-based inference.

Run. Nibi (Digital Research Alliance of Canada), 2026-11-19: enumeration array job 28020100, BLRT array job 28020107, K = 4 analysis job 28022493; the R version (latentmixtures, examples/brfss_ace/brfss_lca.R, job 28023102) reproduces every table to the printed digits.

With n = 136,947 every test rejects K − 1 classes and BIC keeps falling; they cannot choose K here. The choice of K = 4 rests on the size of the BIC improvements (−9,570, −7,148, then −1,081 and −644), the replication of the best log-likelihood (all 400 stage-2 starts up to K = 4; 3 of 400 at K = 5), and the K = 5 solution, which splits off a 6.5% class with P(physical abuse) and P(domestic violence) estimated at 0.000 (boundary estimates; results/runs/enum/enum_K5_profiles.tsv).

Reading the results

  • With n ≈ 137,000 every likelihood-ratio test (LMR, BLRT) rejects K − 1 classes up to K = 6 and BIC keeps falling: in large surveys these criteria measure power, not the number of substantively distinct classes. The choice of K = 4 rests on the size of the BIC gains, the replication of the best log-likelihood and boundary estimates at K = 5.
  • The four classes (low adversity 58%, abuse 17%, household dysfunction 16%, high adversity 10%) separate abuse from household dysfunction, and class membership is strongly graded by age: whether this is cohort change or age-related recall cannot be told from a cross-section.
  • The BCH prevalences are descriptive. Whether preventing abuse would reduce mental distress is a causal question with its own assumptions: see the BRFSS example of LatentClassCausal.jl.
