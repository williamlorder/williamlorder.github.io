Example: BRFSS — interventions on childhood abuse

A real-data run of the intervention estimators on the Behavioral Risk Factor Surveillance System 2022–2024 (CDC; public domain): 121,351 adults from the 24 state-years (15 states) with answers to the adverse childhood experience (ACE) module. What would adult frequent mental distress and depression look like if childhood emotional, physical and sexual abuse had been halved (shift(0.5)) or prevented (setto([0, 0, 0]))? The example uses intervene_effect (AIPW, TMLE, class-based adjustment), intervene_within, intervene_shares, intervene_latent_sensitivity and intervene_latent_outcome. Scripts, Slurm jobs and reproduction steps: examples/brfss_ace/README.md; the latent class enumeration of the same ACE items is the BRFSS example of LatentMixtures.jl.

Illustration, unweighted

The confounders are age, sex, race/ethnicity, year and state only; parental socioeconomic position and family structure are not measured, so conditional exchangeability is not credible and the numbers are not causal estimates of the effect of abuse. The analyses are unweighted and ignore the BRFSS strata and PSUs (the package does not yet take sampling weights or a survey design; causal.jl marks where they go).

The parametric g-formula (200 bootstrap replications, each applying the same 5-fold cross-fitted estimator to the resample, on 121,351 rows; rerun with that bootstrap as array job 28116952, tasks 3, 7, 11 and 15, which left every entry of the table unchanged at four decimals; earlier run: array job 28023586, task 3 rerun as 28074535) gives effects 0.1–0.6 percentage points larger than AIPW/TMLE (e.g. −3.5 vs −3.2 points for preventing abuse, FMD) with smaller bootstrap SEs: it relies entirely on the outcome model, whereas AIPW and TMLE also use the exposure model, so the gap indicates some outcome-model misspecification and the doubly robust estimates are the ones to report.

Halving the reported abuse would lower FMD from 13.2% to about 11.4% (−1.8 points), and preventing it to about 9.9–10.0% (−3.2 points); depression from 19.5% to 17.1% and 15.1%. AIPW and TMLE agree to the third decimal. Adjusting for the latent classes of the five non-intervened ACEs instead of the ACEs themselves (adjust = :classes, K = 3) gives larger effects (−3.6 and −5.1 points for prevention): with only K − 1 = 2 class probabilities, H no longer contains all the confounding information of the five household ACEs, and the larger minimum propensity (0.0014 vs 0.0012) and smaller maximum weights (5.8 vs 17.4 for the shift, 13.0 vs 81.2 for prevention) show the positivity gain that is the motivation for this option. Full prevention puts the whole intervention mass on the all-zero abuse profile, hence the maximum weight of 81.

The class-size weighted average of the class effects is the population effect (e.g. FMD, shift: −0.017848 in both). Most of the population effect comes from the abuse and high-adversity classes, where the intervened items are common.

Post-intervention classification shares Δk = πk(g) − πk(natural) (`interveneshares`): shift(0.5) +0.053, +0.045, −0.062, −0.037; prevention +0.107, +0.098, −0.131, −0.075 for classes 1–4. These are not causal effects on the class (which precedes and generates the ACEs); they are how the class model would classify the post-intervention profiles.

The SEs above condition on the class model. The bootstrap that refits the class model and the nuisance models (PART=boot, FMD, shift(0.5), B = 200, 64 cores; job 28025570) was still running after 1 h 31 min when this README was written; its output will be results/boot_fmd.tsv. Until then, read the conditional SEs as lower bounds: in the simulation of the Latent classes and interventions page, intervals for the shares that hold the class model fixed covered about 45% (bootstrap: about 95%).
